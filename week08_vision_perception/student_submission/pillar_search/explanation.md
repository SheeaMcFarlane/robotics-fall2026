# pillar_search

## diagnosis
In the blue stage the pillar first appeared at the left edge of the camera image, with a normalized error of −0.98. The mask showed one clean white shape on the pillar, so the correct contour was selected and the centroid sat on it. A negative error means the target is on the left. The controller negates the error, so it commanded a positive angular velocity up to +0.45 rad/s, which turns the robot left. As the pillar moved toward the middle of the image, the error shrank to −0.12, and the turn command shrank with it. In the saved frame the centroid is just left of the center line about −0.06, which is inside the 0.10 tolerance. The robot then confirmed three centered frames and held still. I changed the steering gain from 1.2 to 0.8. It improved smoothness: no color crossed zero error, whereas at gain 1.2 green and turquoise overshot and ended on the right side of center, green at +0.07. It made the run slower, each centering phase was longer, and the total went from 18.99 s to 20.62 s. It also left the robot stopping nearer the edge of the tolerance −0.05 to −0.08 instead of close to 0, because the smaller commands near the target let it stop as soon as it enters the tolerance band.

## evidence
All four colors were centered (red, green, turquoise and blue), and none were missed. The state timeline shows each color went through SEARCH, CENTER, CONFIRM, HOLD and NEXT, with COMPLETE after blue. During SEARCH the robot rotated at a constant 0.35 rad/s until the pillar appeared, and each pillar entered from the left edge with an error near −1, so the controller commanded a positive angular velocity, a left turn, each time while the error shrank into the 0.10 tolerance. The camera evidence agrees: the mask is a single clean contour on the pillar, with the centroid close to the center line, and the linear speed was 0 throughout, so the robot never drove forward. However, a centered colored region and a stable hold do not prove object identity or distance. The controller only checks that the largest region in a color range sits near the image center, so a different object of a similar color would pass the same test and color is not identity. The robot only rotates, and the centroid’s horizontal position gives a direction rather than a range, so a small near pillar and a large far pillar can produce the same centered blob. A stable hold shows only that the error stayed within tolerance while the robot stood still, meaning the control loop is steady, and it says nothing about whether the target is the right object or where it is in the world.
